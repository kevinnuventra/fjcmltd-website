2. Information We Collect

Depending on your interaction with us, we may collect:

  • Names, contact details and postal addresses.
  • Business names, job titles and details of your relationship with an organisation.
  • Enquiries, instructions, appointments and correspondence.
  • Billing, payment, bank and account information.
  • Marketing preferences, opt-outs and objections.

For debt recovery and credit control, information may include:

  • Creditor, debtor and guarantor details.
  • Balances, invoices, contracts, statements and due dates.
  • Payment and contact history.
  • Disputes, financial circumstances and supporting evidence.
  • Tracing results and relevant insolvency or court information.
  • Records of steps taken on a case.

For business prospecting, we may record legal entity details, company number, sector, location, published business facts, business contact details, source URLs and verification dates. Pure company information is not personal data unless it relates to an identifiable living person.

Website information may include IP address, browser and device details, access and security logs, and information collected through cookies or similar technologies where these are used.

3. Sources of Information

We obtain information directly through forms, instructions, calls, emails, post and service interactions.

For casework, sources may include our instructing client, a debtor or their representative, solicitors, tracing and credit reference providers, insolvency records, The Gazette, public registers and other relevant lawful sources.

For marketing, sources include public business websites, Companies House and business directories located through online research.

Where we obtain personal information from another source, we provide privacy information within a reasonable period and no later than one month, or at first communication or disclosure if earlier, unless a lawful exception applies. For prospecting, we record the source and can identify where your contact details came from.

4. Why We Use Information and Our Lawful Bases

We select a lawful basis for each purpose before processing personal information. The basis depends on the activity and our role. A client's contract with us does not automatically provide a contract basis for processing someone else's information.

Legitimate interests: We use necessary information to assess and manage instructions, verify relevant identities and contact details, pursue or resolve debts, communicate about accounts, support tracing and legal claims, manage organisational client and supplier relationships, respond to enquiries and complaints, protect systems and prevent fraud.

The interests are our clients' recovery of money owed, the fair resolution of accounts and disputes, and our operation and protection of the business. We assess necessity and balance these interests against individuals' rights and circumstances.

Contract: Where you personally enter into a contract with us, or ask us to take steps towards one, we use information necessary for that contract or those requested steps. For staff representing corporate clients, we generally rely on legitimate interests to manage the business relationship.

Legal obligation: We process information where necessary to meet a specific legal duty, such as tax and accounting requirements or a binding court requirement. Contractual duties alone are not legal obligations for this purpose.

Consent: We rely on consent for activities that require it, including electronic marketing or cookies where applicable. You can withdraw consent at any time without affecting the lawfulness of earlier processing. Business marketing is explained in section 6.

5. Debt Recovery and Sensitive Information

We use relevant case information to assess the instruction, check balances and evidence, contact the appropriate parties, consider disputes and payment proposals, manage recovery activity and support legal proceedings where instructed.

We share necessary progress information with the instructing client. We take reasonable steps to check accuracy and consider corrections and disputes.

You do not generally have to consent to necessary debt recovery processing where another lawful basis applies. You can still exercise your rights, including objecting to processing based on legitimate interests. We assess those objections in accordance with the law; they do not automatically require all case processing to stop.

If relevant health or other special category information is needed to understand circumstances or handle a matter fairly, we require both an Article 6 lawful basis and an appropriate Article 9 condition. Depending on the purpose, this may be explicit consent or necessity for establishing, exercising or defending legal claims. These conditions are not assumed to apply to every case.

Where criminal offence information is processed, we also need the appropriate Article 10 and Data Protection Act 2018 authorisation or condition. We restrict collection, access and sharing to what is necessary.

Please provide only relevant information and use the agreed secure channel for sensitive documents.

Information requested for a service is voluntary unless we explain a legal or contractual requirement. Without necessary information we may be unable to assess an instruction or provide a requested service. Debtor information may also be obtained lawfully from other sources.

6. Business Research and Marketing

We research organisations that may benefit from our credit control and debt recovery services. We check the relationship between the website, published contact details and legal entity. We do not treat information being public as permission for unrestricted use.

We may send relevant introductory emails to corporate subscribers, including limited companies and limited liability partnerships, without prior consent under the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR).

For councils, schools and trusts, eligibility depends on the legal entity and subscriber status. Named work contacts remain personal data; where appropriate, we rely on assessed legitimate interests in introducing relevant services and developing business relationships.

For sole traders and other individual subscribers, we require consent unless a valid statutory exception applies. Uncertain subscriber types require verification before unsolicited emailing.

7. Your Right to Stop Marketing

You have an absolute right to object to our use of your personal information for direct marketing, including related profiling.

Use the opt-out function in our emails or email customer@fjcmltd.co.uk. We stop the marketing covered by your objection and honour unsubscribe requests from generic business inboxes.

We keep the minimum contact and objection details on a suppression list to prevent further marketing, including where the same details are found again through research.

An opt-out does not prevent necessary service, account, debt recovery or legal communications that are not marketing.

8. n8n and Automated Research

We use n8n to operate a workflow that assists with public business research, verification and preparation of draft introductory emails.

An authorised connection to our Microsoft 365 mailbox saves draft emails in Outlook for review before sending. The workflow operates separately from our website hosting.

Research may group prospects by characteristics such as sector, location and company size and use relevant published business facts to personalise an introduction. This supports marketing selection.

The prospecting workflow does not make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on individuals.

Information passed through the workflow may include business contact details, public website content, source links and draft correspondence. The information received by n8n's underlying service providers depends on the enabled features and account configuration. Section 10 addresses overseas processing.

9. Who We Share Information With

We share relevant information only where necessary and lawful. Recipients may include:

  • Authorised staff, contractors and representatives.
  • The instructing creditor or client.
  • A debtor's authorised representative.
  • Solicitors, barristers and other legal advisers.
  • Tracing agents and investigation providers.
  • Credit reference agencies.
  • Enforcement agents.
  • Accountants, insurers and professional advisers.
  • Payment providers.
  • Courts, regulators, public authorities or law enforcement where legally justified.

Technology recipients include website hosting and security providers, Microsoft 365 email and document services, n8n workflow services and business record storage providers, together with applicable service subprocessors.

We use appropriate contractual arrangements where providers process information on our behalf. Some professional or specialist recipients act as independent controllers and their own privacy information applies to their activities.

We do not sell personal information.

10. Processing Outside the United Kingdom

Our technology providers may process or access personal information outside the United Kingdom. The locations depend on the services and features used, including any applicable support access and subprocessors. We check the relevant provider arrangements and apply the transfer requirements described below.

Where required, transfers rely on applicable UK adequacy regulations or appropriate safeguards, such as the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or EU Standard Contractual Clauses with the UK Addendum, together with the required assessment.

You can contact us for details of the locations and safeguards relevant to your information and how to obtain a copy of the safeguards, subject to necessary redactions.

11. Security

We use technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risks, including access restrictions and secure systems and transmission for personal information.

We limit access to those who need it for their work and require appropriate confidentiality and security from relevant providers.

No system is entirely risk free. Please contact us if you believe information has been sent to the wrong person or handled insecurely.

12. How Long We Retain Information

We retain personal information only while there is a justified need. The criteria depend on the category and purpose:

  • Debt and service records: The period needed to manage the account, instruction and any payment arrangement; the time reasonably needed to handle complaints, disputes and legal claims; and applicable accounting or other statutory requirements. Closure of a case does not always require immediate deletion, but it does not justify indefinite retention.
  • Enquiries and business relationships: Whether the enquiry remains active or leads to a service, the duration of the relationship, and any relevant complaint, recordkeeping or legal claim requirements.
  • Prospect records: Continuing relevance of the offer, accuracy and age of the information, contact history and any objection. We remove information when there is no continuing justified marketing purpose.
  • Suppression records: As long as needed to prevent renewed unwanted marketing.
  • Website logs and workflow records: Operational, troubleshooting and security needs, the sensitivity of the information and configured deletion settings.

We review retention and securely delete or anonymise information when no longer needed. Restricted archiving is still retention and requires a justified purpose. A legal hold may extend retention until the relevant matter is resolved.

13. Your Rights

Depending on the circumstances, you may have the right to:

  • Request access to your personal information.
  • Request correction of inaccurate or incomplete information.
  • Request erasure of your information.
  • Request restriction of processing.
  • Object to processing based on legitimate interests.
  • Receive certain information you provided in a portable format where processing is automated and based on consent or contract.
  • Withdraw consent where we rely on it.

Your direct marketing objection is explained separately in section 7. Other rights are subject to legal conditions and exemptions, including where records remain necessary for legal claims.

Contact customer@fjcmltd.co.uk to exercise a right. We may request proportionate information to verify identity or clarify the request.

We normally respond within one month. Where a lawful extension or adjustment applies, we explain it. Requests are normally free. Where we act only as a processor, we help the relevant client respond.

14. Cookies and Similar Technologies

Cookies and similar technologies may support website functions, security and, where used, analytics.

We obtain consent before using technologies that require it. Where a statutory exception applies, we meet its conditions, including providing information and an objection mechanism where required.

Browser controls alone do not replace consent where the law requires it. Browser settings can help you manage cookies. You can contact us for information about the technologies used on our website and the choices available.
